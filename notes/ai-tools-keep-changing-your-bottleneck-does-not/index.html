AI tools keep changing. Your bottleneck doesn't.

A few more AI updates landed this week. What I want to tell businesses in Malaysia and Singapore is not which tool to pick up first. Tools change every month. Your bottleneck does not.

16 November 2026 · By Hunter Foo

This week (10–16 November 2026) brought a few more AI updates. After going through them, what I want to tell businesses in Malaysia and Singapore is not which tool to adopt first. Tools change every month. Your bottleneck does not.

Businesses in ChatGPT: an AI can only repeat what it can read

OpenAI has released a beta called Businesses in ChatGPT. Businesses can claim their profile; those that qualify can also build an agent to answer customer questions. It is not confirmed to be open in Malaysia and Singapore.

Even if it opens later, the core point does not change: an AI only repeats the information it can read. If your service pages, FAQs, and Google Business Profile — address, hours, and what you do — are unclear, a smarter ChatGPT still cannot invent an accurate answer. Getting the website and Google Business Profile right matters more than waiting for a feature to arrive here.

Sierra Poppy 1.0: it does not matter whether the buyer is a person or an AI

Sierra has released Poppy 1.0, which lets an AI assistant act for the user when dealing with a business. Shopify and Wix have it on by default.

If you sell products or provide a service, the visitor may soon be a customer's AI assistant, not only a person. But a buyer who is a person and a buyer who is an AI still need the same things: what you offer, what it costs, and whether it is in stock. If that is clear on the page, they can decide. You do not have to chase a new protocol first. Put those basics where they can be found.

a16z's agent economy: the job of a website is to help you get chosen

a16z says AI agents already make up most of the web's traffic, and puts forward the idea of an agent economy. I do not have my own figures on whether agents are already in the majority; I am passing on their claim.

What businesses should take seriously is the next layer: more of the comparing, shortlisting, and ordering will be done by machines. The job of a website is no longer only to be read by people. It is to help you get chosen. Write your FAQ and service pages so a machine can understand them too: what you do, what you do not do, how to reach you, and what the next step is. If that is clear, a machine can recommend you. If it is vague, you are easier to skip.

Baidu KOOKO: writing software is getting cheaper; knowing what to build is not

According to a third-party account, Baidu's KOOKO has been shown generating a website, a booking system, and an admin backend through chat. It is not confirmed to be available outside China.

Even if it becomes available later, this does not change: writing software is getting cheaper; knowing what should be built has not. A tool can quickly put up a booking page or a backend. Who your customers are, which process to fix first, who follows up after it goes live, and what you do when something goes wrong — those still need a person to think through. Get the business clear, then choose the tool.

Tools change. The bottleneck does not.

Look at these four updates together, and the same sentence appears: tools change every month; your bottleneck does not. The bottleneck is usually still this: people cannot find you, cannot understand you, or do not know how to reach you next.
